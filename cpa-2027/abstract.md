# Extending the Visualization of Categorical Data in R

When visualizing categorical data in R, psychologists have often needed to leave a typical ggplot2 (Wickham, 2016) workflow to take full advantage of displays designed for such data. By leaving ggplot2, researchers are forced to learn a new system for visualizing their data; simultaneously, they lose access to simpler and more familiar ways of customizing their displays. Thus, we present two recently developed open-source packages that address this, using classic data sets to illustrate their utility. First, ggfourfold brings (stratified) fourfold displays of the sample odds ratio(s) to ggplot2. ggfourfold also extends the presentation of these displays, most notably by providing the option to illustrate cell sizes using the area of squares rather than quarter-circles. Second, ggmosaic2 extends the mosaic plots of ggmosaic (Jeppson & Hofmann, 2023), providing the ability to shade tiles based on the residuals from a fitted loglinear model, thereby illustrating a pattern of association that is separate from cell size. Other improvements made in ggmosaic2 include a simplified interface, labelling, and faceting. Since each package provides ggplot2 layers, researchers can both stick with and take advantage of a familiar workflow, making it easier to see, check, and communicate associations in categorical data.
